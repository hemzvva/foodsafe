PRIMARY DISH SET

AnorelArts · Watercolor Cooking Set

35 dishes · 48 ingredients · 12 utensils · 500×500 assets
Complete set listed at £12.50 minimum; dish-only pack £4 minimum. Creator says commercial/personal use allowed, attribution optional; AI-assisted. Ignore the fantasy backgrounds; use only individual transparent food art.

Style fit: 4/5 for dishes. Cohesive watercolor coverage and usable resolution. It is more saturated and has heavier outlines than the source; select only candidates that remain close at the actual card size.

Winner for complete dish coverage and consistency. Download and audition the actual PNGs before replacing existing food art.

REJECTED FOR CORE ART

PictArts · free hand-drawn spaghetti

Transparent PNG · commercial use advertised as free
Official terms prohibit raw redistribution and describe app use as a design component; do not bundle it as a raw asset collection. Confirm rights if you want food art to be a central part of the product.

Style fit: 2/5. Thick dark comic outlines and a prominent white sticker halo do not match FoodSafe's softer pencil/watercolor shape.

Do not use this for dish cards. The style is too graphic and the license wording is less suitable for a food-asset-heavy app.

Final recommendation

What I would actually choose: AnorelArts for coherent whole-dish art + The Rogue Cookie for the individual ingredient drawings, paired with static Drawably outlines and static Doodle Icons for interface symbols. Use one handwritten type family globally. These are researched, visually inspected candidates—not installed assets yet. The food sources must be downloaded under their stated licenses, then rendered at the actual 32–180 px image sizes and compared to the source before final approval.
Offline rule: this audition page may load external images for visual review. The real FoodSafe app must bundle the licensed assets locally; there should be no remote image URLs in the shipped app. If an external preview fails to load, use its official source link above. Candidate scores are subjective design judgments, not automated similarity measurements.
